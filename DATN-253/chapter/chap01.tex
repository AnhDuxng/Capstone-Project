\chapter{Introduction and Overview}

\section{Problem Statement and Motivation}

Academic credentials (e.g., diplomas, degrees, certificates, and academic transcripts) serve as fundamental attestations of an individual's knowledge, skills, and qualifications. In modern hiring and education pipelines, these documents represent critical evidence for employers and academic institutions during candidate evaluations. However, the legacy certificate issuance and verification ecosystem suffers from severe structural vulnerabilities:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Credential Forgery and Fraud}: Physical paper degrees and unauthenticated scanned PDFs can be easily duplicated, manipulated, or fabricated using modern digital editing and printing tools.
    \item \textbf{High Verification Overhead and Lack of Direct Verifiability}: Cross-institution verification often requires manual inquiries, physical stamp verifications, or dependence on centralized registrar portals, which are slow, costly, and subject to single-point-of-failure bottlenecks.
    \item \textbf{Over-disclosure of Sensitive Information}: Traditional certificates mandate revealing the entire document (including Personally Identifiable Information (PII) such as date of birth, student ID, detailed GPA) even when a verifier only needs to assert whether the candidate graduated.
\end{itemize}

The \textbf{BK Credential System} is engineered as a three-tier hybrid architecture combining a relational database, an on-chain blockchain trust anchor, and decentralized storage (IPFS), enabling fully trustless verification without relying on centralized backend servers. The system addresses these challenges by guaranteeing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tamper-evidence}: Enforced through cryptographic hashing and public blockchain state anchoring.
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Selective Disclosure}: Permitting credential holders to disclose only required attributes without compromising signature validity.
    \item \textbf{Cost and Scalability Efficiency}: Utilizing Merkle tree batching to amortize blockchain transaction costs across thousands of graduating students.
    \item \textbf{Trustless Verification}: Enabling any third party to cryptographically verify credentials directly against blockchain trust anchors without depending on proprietary university backend infrastructure.
\end{itemize}

In Phase 2, we designed and implemented a functional prototype leveraging \textit{SD-JWT VC (Selective Disclosure JSON Web Token Verifiable Credential)} paired with an on-chain \textit{Merkle Tree} registry. However, rigorous evaluation at university scale ($\geq$10,000 graduates per cohort) revealed two significant operational bottlenecks:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{IPFS Operational Overhead}: Mandatory pinning of Public Canonical Views (PCV) per credential to IPFS incurred recurring storage fees (\$0.001--\$0.01/credential/month) and introduced an operational dependency on external IPFS gateways.
    \item \textbf{SD-JWT Library Complexity}: Lack of native Web3 standard support for the ES256K curve in standard JWT libraries necessitated custom SD-JWT implementations with substantial JWT envelope and \texttt{\_sd\_alg} parsing overhead.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of Phase 3}

Rather than serving as a linear incremental update, Phase 3 represents a fundamental architectural paradigm shift. It is formulated to validate the core design thesis: \textit{at institutional university scale, a decoupled architecture where the blockchain functions strictly as a Trust Anchor and cryptographic signatures are executed off-chain via EIP-712 achieves equivalent security and privacy guarantees while drastically minimizing operational costs and operational complexity}.

The technical scope of Phase 3 is structured across five systematic engineering layers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cryptographic and Protocol Layer}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Formal specification of the EIP-712 typed structured data schema, including domain separation parameters (\texttt{name}, \texttt{version}, \texttt{chainId}, \texttt{verifyingContract}) and nested data structures (\texttt{PublicClaims}, \texttt{BkCredential}).
        \item Design of a 256-bit salted-hash commitment scheme for privacy-preserving Selective Disclosure, computing attribute commitments as $C_i = \text{keccak256}(\text{abi.encode}(\text{salt}_i, \text{key}_i, \text{value}_i))$ directly within the signed EIP-712 payload.
        \item Integration of OpenZeppelin \texttt{StandardMerkleTree} with double keccak256 leaf hashing ($\text{leaf} = \text{keccak256}(\text{keccak256}(\text{abi.encode}(\dots)))$) to prevent second-preimage collision attacks.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Smart Contract and Trust Anchor Layer}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Implementation and verification of \texttt{IssuerRegistry.sol}, governing authorized signing addresses, decentralized identifiers (DIDs), and two-step ownership management via OpenZeppelin \texttt{Ownable2Step}.
        \item Implementation of \texttt{CredentialRegistryV3.sol}, functioning as the on-chain Trust Anchor by recording batch Merkle root commitments via \texttt{anchorBatch()} and managing credential revocations through an optimized 256-bit bitmap mapping (\texttt{uint256[]}), supported by \texttt{Pausable} emergency controls.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Software Development Kit (SDK) Layer}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Engineering of a standalone, modular TypeScript SDK (\texttt{@bkcred/sdk}) comprising 6 specialized packages: \texttt{domain}, \texttt{signer}, \texttt{verifier}, \texttt{disclosure}, \texttt{merkle}, and barrel export \texttt{index}.
        \item Formulation of an offline-capable, deterministic \textbf{10-Step Verification Pipeline} combining local schema validation, cryptographic struct hashing, commitment checking, Merkle proof evaluation, and online smart contract assertions.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Full-Stack Application and Portal Layer}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Construction of 4 dedicated web portals on Next.js 14 (App Router) and PostgreSQL (Prisma ORM): (1) \textit{Issuer Portal} for batch CSV parsing, off-chain bulk signing, and on-chain root anchoring; (2) \textit{Holder Portal} featuring passwordless email authentication (Privy) and an interactive Selective Disclosure customization interface; (3) \textit{Public Verifier Portal} supporting direct JSON file validation and deep-link routing (\texttt{/v3/verify?credId=\dots}); and (4) \textit{Administrative Portal} for signer key lifecycle management.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation and Benchmarking Layer}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Development of automated testing harness and fixture generators capable of producing cohorts of 10,000 synthetic graduate credentials.
        \item Execution of multi-scenario benchmark suites evaluating offline signing throughput, end-to-end issuance latency, concurrent verification capacity, gas profiling, and static security analysis using the Slither framework.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

This graduation thesis presents the architectural design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of the BK Credential System Phase 3.

\section{Contributions of This Thesis}

To establish a clear boundary for our work, this section outlines the foundational components inherited from Phase 2 and presents the specific engineering contributions achieved in Phase 3.

\subsection{Components Inherited from Phase 2}

Phase 2 laid the groundwork for verifiable academic credentials at our university, providing key architectural foundations that Phase 3 builds upon:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Three-Tier Hybrid Architecture}: The overarching architectural model combining a relational database for application state, an on-chain blockchain registry for trust anchoring, and off-chain storage.
    \item \textbf{On-Chain Merkle Batch Anchoring}: Phase 2 introduced batch issuance via Merkle trees, establishing an initial baseline of $\sim$24,638 gas per credential at a batch size of $N=100$~\cite{Grech2017BlockchainInEducation}.
    \item \textbf{Concept of Selective Disclosure}: Phase 2 introduced holder-side attribute privacy using the SD-JWT (Selective Disclosure for JSON Web Tokens) format.
    \item \textbf{Role-Based Smart Contracts}: Phase 2 deployed \texttt{IssuerRegistry} and \texttt{CredentialRegistry} contracts leveraging OpenZeppelin \texttt{AccessControl} to separate administrative and signing privileges.
    \item \textbf{Walletless Holder Experience}: Phase 2 adopted passwordless magic-link email authentication, allowing students to access credentials without managing Web3 wallets or paying transaction fees.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Contributions of Phase 3}

Building upon the Phase 2 baseline, our work in Phase 3 addresses identified scalability, operational, and architectural limitations through the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Off-Chain EIP-712 Issuance Architecture}: Designed and implemented an off-chain issuance model based on EIP-712 typed structured data signatures, achieving zero gas consumption per individual credential while ensuring native compatibility with Web3 wallets and transparent signature inspection.
    
    \item \textbf{Salted-Hash Selective Disclosure Mechanism}: Formulated a lightweight selective disclosure mechanism computing per-attribute commitments as $C_i = \text{keccak256}(\text{abi.encode}(\text{salt}_i, \text{key}_i, \text{value}_i))$ directly within the EIP-712 data payload, eliminating the need for specialized JWT parsing libraries.
    
    \item \textbf{Space-Efficient On-Chain Bitmap Revocation}: Implemented a gas-optimized revocation mechanism in Solidity that packs 256 credential revocation flags into a single \texttt{uint256} storage slot, significantly reducing on-chain storage costs compared to traditional per-credential boolean mappings.
    
    \item \textbf{Chunked High-Throughput Issuance Pipeline}: Developed an asynchronous bulk issuance engine capable of processing university-scale cohorts ($10{,}000$ credentials divided into chunks of $500$) with real-time Server-Sent Events (SSE) streaming, preventing Node.js event-loop blocking and memory exhaustion.
    
    \item \textbf{Deterministic 10-Step Verification Workflow}: Formulated and implemented a standardized 10-step verification algorithm (combining 6 offline cryptographic checks, 3 on-chain registry validations, and 1 expiration check) packaged as an open-source, modular TypeScript SDK (\texttt{@bkcred/sdk}).
    
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Benchmarking and Security Analysis}: Conducted systematic empirical benchmarks across five evaluation scenarios (Scenarios A--E) evaluating issuance throughput, verification latency, gas scaling, concurrent server load, and static smart contract vulnerability analysis with Slither.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Comparative Analysis with Phase 2 Architecture}

To clarify the motivation for transitioning to Phase 3, this section contrasts our proposed design with the previous Phase 2 prototype.

\textbf{Overview of Phase 2 Architecture}: The Phase 2 prototype adopted an SD-JWT VC (Selective Disclosure JSON Web Token Verifiable Credential) model integrated with an on-chain Merkle tree registry. It demonstrated batch anchoring to aggregate multiple credentials into a single Merkle root ($\sim$24,638 gas per credential for $N=100$), utilized two smart contracts with role-based access control, and provided passwordless email authentication for students without requiring individual Web3 wallets.

While Phase 2 validated the concept of decentralized academic credentials, several operational challenges emerged when considering production university deployment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operational Dependency on IPFS}: Storing each credential's Public Canonical View (PCV) on IPFS incurred recurring third-party pinning fees and introduced potential verification failures whenever external IPFS gateways experienced latency or downtime.
    \item \textbf{Ecosystem Interoperability Overhead}: Implementing SD-JWT over the secp256k1 curve (ES256K) required custom decoding logic and non-standard JWT formatting, increasing client-side bundle size and maintenance complexity.
    \item \textbf{Revocation Storage Costs}: Recording revocations via individual mapping entries (\texttt{mapping(bytes32 => bool)}) allocated a new 32-byte storage slot for every revoked credential ($\sim$103,154 gas per revocation).
    \item \textbf{Limited Evaluation at Scale}: Phase 2 was primarily evaluated on small batch samples ($N \le 100$), leaving system performance unverified under large graduating cohorts ($N = 10{,}000$) and concurrent verification loads.
\end{itemize}

In Phase 3, we address these challenges by adopting an off-chain EIP-712 structured data model anchored to an optimized on-chain registry:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Off-Chain Issuance}: Individual credentials exist as self-contained JSON documents signed off-chain via standard EIP-712 typed structured data, incurring zero gas cost for individual certificate issuance.
    \item \textbf{Amortized Batch Anchoring}: Only the Merkle root representing the cohort is committed on-chain through a single \texttt{anchorBatch()} transaction ($\sim$167,804 gas on Sepolia), amortizing the cost to approximately 16.8 gas per credential for a cohort of $10{,}000$.
    \item \textbf{Independent Verification}: Credential holders directly retain their signed JSON files, eliminating mandatory IPFS dependencies and recurring pinning fees for basic verification.
    \item \textbf{EVM-Native Selective Disclosure}: Attribute masking is accomplished through salted Keccak-256 commitments directly within the typed struct, simplifying verification logic.
    \item \textbf{Compact Bitmap Revocation}: Revocation flags are indexed into 256-bit words (\texttt{uint256[]}), achieving a 256$\times$ reduction in storage slot allocation.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2 (Related Work and Theoretical Foundations)}: Surveys existing blockchain-based academic credential systems (BlockCerts, OpenCerts, EBSI) to establish a comparative baseline, followed by the theoretical and cryptographic foundations of our work, including Verifiable Credentials, EIP-712 structured data hashing, Merkle tree batching, salted selective disclosure, and space-efficient bitmap revocation.

    \item \textbf{Chapter 4 (System Implementation)}: Documents the engineering realization of the Solidity 0.8.20 smart contract suite, the 6-module TypeScript SDK, four interactive web application portals (Admin, Issuer, Holder, and Verifier), and the chunked asynchronous bulk issuance engine.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 5 (Testing and Empirical Evaluation)}: Presents empirical benchmark results across Scenarios A through E (scaling up to 10,000 credentials), on-chain transaction measurements on Ethereum Sepolia, static security analysis with Slither, and threat modeling evaluated against STRIDE security test cases.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 6 (Project Evaluation, Limitations, and Future Directions)}: Summarizes completed milestones across the 15-week timeline, provides a systematic analysis of system limitations and engineering trade-offs (including signer revocation consistency, holder-binding presentation boundaries, and computational bottlenecks), and outlines future research extensions including W3C VC alignment, Layer~2 rollups, and zero-knowledge proofs.
    \item \textbf{References}: Complete bibliography of cited literature and technical standards.
    \item \textbf{Appendix}: Contains supplementary materials, including the Ethereum Sepolia deployment manifest, public source code repository references, and a glossary of technical terms.
\end{itemize}
